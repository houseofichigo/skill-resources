# Lineup snapshot — 22 September 2026

**This file is stale by design.** It exists so the skill still works offline
and so you can recognise the *shape* of each provider's ladder. Verify every
name and every price by search before repeating it to anyone. Providers have
been shipping new tiers roughly monthly.

If you are reading this more than a couple of months after the date above,
assume most specifics below are wrong.

---

## The ladder, by provider

Most provider lineups can be grouped into three practical rungs, even when a
provider exposes more than three named models.

| Rung | OpenAI | Anthropic | Google |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fast / light** | GPT-5.6 Luna | Claude Haiku 4.5 | Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite |
| **Balanced / everyday** | GPT-5.6 Terra | Claude Sonnet 5 | Gemini 3.8 Flash |
| **Frontier / deep** | GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Astra | Claude Opus 5, Claude Fable 5.1 | Gemini 3.1 Pro, Gemini 3 Deep Think |

Notes as of the snapshot date:

- **OpenAI** — GPT-5.6 ships as a family (Sol flagship, Terra balanced, Luna
  cheapest), with GPT-6 Astra above it as the most capable general model,
  surfaced in ChatGPT as GPT-6 Pro. In ChatGPT the family is hidden behind a
  thinking control: Instant / Medium / High / Extra High, plus a Pro option.
  Free and Go tiers default to Luna.
- **Anthropic** — Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 5, Opus 5, Fable 5.1, in ascending
  capability. Fable is positioned for the hardest work and long-running agents;
  Opus for complex agentic coding and computer use; Sonnet for everyday
  professional work.
- **Google** — the Flash line moves fastest: Gemini 3.8 Flash arrived
  2 September 2026, the fourth Flash upgrade in four months, and is positioned
  as the most intelligent Flash-tier model. Pro sits at 3.1; Deep Think is the
  maximum-reasoning mode in the Gemini apps and requires a top subscription
  tier. Flash pricing carried an introductory rate through 31 December 2026
  that roughly doubles on 1 January 2027 — a good example of why prices must be
  looked up, not remembered.

## Consumer product modes

Non-technical users meet modes, not model names. These matter more than the
model ladder for anyone working in a chat window.

| Mode | What it is | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Instant / default | The fast tier answering directly | Everyday questions |
| Thinking / extended | The same or a higher tier, given more time to reason | Real professional work |
| Maximum / Pro / Deep Think | The deepest reasoning available, slow and usually gated to top plans | Hard problems only |
| Deep research | A multi-minute agent that browses many sources and returns a cited report | Anything that would otherwise be an afternoon of searching |
| Coding agent | Reads a repository, edits files, runs tests | Any repo-scale work |

## Rough economics

Indicative only. Verify before quoting.

- There is no reliable cross-provider multiplier between the three rungs.
- Within the dated lineup above, some adjacent tiers differ by about 2× while
  others differ by 10× or more.
- Reasoning-heavy modes burn far more output tokens than their headline price
  suggests, so a "cheap" model in high-thinking mode can cost more than a
  pricier model answering directly.
- Cached input is dramatically cheaper on most providers; batch processing is
  typically about half price. Both matter at volume.

Because of the reasoning-token effect, **price per million tokens is not
comparable across providers without knowing how many tokens each one actually
spends on your task.** Look up current prices and measure on a real task before
deciding.

## What changed recently, as a sense of pace

Between roughly April and September 2026: OpenAI shipped a whole new family
naming scheme and then a generation above it; Google shipped four Flash
upgrades in four months and changed pricing dates twice; Anthropic added a tier
above its previous flagship. Several strong open-weight models closed most of
the gap on coding.

Which is the argument for the skill's core rule: search before you name
anything.
